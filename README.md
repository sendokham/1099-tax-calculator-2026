## 📐 2026 IRS Self-Employment Tax Rules & Rates

### 1. Self-Employment Tax (SECA)
Independent contractors, sole proprietors, and freelancers pay both employee and employer portions of Social Security and Medicare:

* **Taxable Base**: Calculated on **92.35%** of your net Schedule C business profit.
* **Social Security (OASDI)**: **12.4%** capped at the 2026 Social Security Wage Base ($176,100).
* **Medicare**: **2.9%** on 100% of net self-employment earnings (uncapped).
* **Additional Medicare Tax**: **0.9%** on earnings exceeding $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly).
* **Combined Base SECA Rate**: **15.3%**.

### 2. Above-the-Line Deductions
Contractors deduct **50% of their total SE tax** directly from adjusted gross income (AGI) prior to calculating standard or itemized federal income taxes.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### What happens if I don't pay quarterly taxes?
The IRS assesses an underpayment penalty under IRC Section 6654 if you owe more than $1,000 at tax time and did not pay at least 90% of your current year liability or 100% of your prior year liability (safe harbor rule).

### How do I calculate standard mileage deductions?
For 2026, the standard IRS business mileage rate allows contractors to deduct vehicle depreciation and fuel costs directly from Schedule C gross revenues before calculating self-employment tax.
